Stop paying for clicks that cannot convert: a weekly search-term review

A simple weekly routine for finding wasted spend in your Amazon search-term report, with a way to decide when a term has had enough chances.

What you'll learn

  • Wasted spend hides in search terms, not in the keywords you chose.
  • Decide how many clicks a term gets from your own conversion rate, not from a rule of thumb.
  • Negatives are protective, but review them too so you do not block real demand.

The search-term report shows the actual words shoppers typed before clicking your ad. It is the most honest document in your account, and it is usually where the most avoidable waste sits.

1Set a click budget per term from your own conversion rate

Click budget ≈ (1 ÷ conversion rate) × 2Example: 10% conversion → about 10 clicks per order → judge a term after ~20 clicks

If your product converts about 10% of clicks into orders, you would expect roughly one order per ten clicks. A term that has taken two or three times that many clicks with no order has had a fair chance. The same term with only four clicks has not.

Work this out per product. A high-priced item with a low conversion rate needs far more clicks before you can judge a term than a cheap, easy-to-buy one.

2The weekly routine

  1. Export the last 14 to 30 days of search terms for your discovery and broad campaigns.
  2. Sort by spend. Start with the terms that cost the most; small amounts can wait.
  3. Mark winners: terms converting at or better than your target ACOS. These graduate to your exact campaign.
  4. Mark losers: terms past their click budget with no orders. Add them as negative exact (or negative phrase if the whole idea is wrong).
  5. Mark the undecided: not enough data yet. Leave them and look again next week.

3Three kinds of waste to look for

  • Irrelevant intent: the shopper wanted something your product is not (wrong size, type or use).
  • Competitor brand names that you did not choose to target and that rarely convert.
  • Overlap: the same term bidding in two of your own campaigns, so you compete with yourself.

4Be careful with negatives

Watch outSeasonal terms can look dead in the off-season and be valuable in-season. Log every negative with the date and the reason, and review the log each quarter.

A negative keyword is a permanent door closed until someone reopens it. Prefer negative exact over negative phrase when you are unsure, keep a log of what you added and why, and review the log every quarter. Seasonal terms in particular can look dead in the off-season and be valuable in-season.

This is the kind of habit that separates an account that gets steadily cleaner from one that just keeps spending.

Try it yourself

Put the lesson to work

Pull 30 days of search terms. For your top five products, work out a click budget (1 ÷ conversion rate × 2) and mark every term that is past it with no orders. Add those as negatives and log the date and reason.
